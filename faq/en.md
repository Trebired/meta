The questions that arrive most often, answered the way they would be answered in an email.

## Working together

### What does it cost

One price for the whole of the work you described, agreed in writing before anything starts, and it does not move unless you change what you asked for. The <!--tbf:link /services-->services<!--/tbf:link--> page carries the published prices and <!--tbf:link /pricing-->pricing<!--/tbf:link--> explains how they are arrived at.

### How long does it take

It depends on the work, and you get a date with the quote rather than after it. What does not happen is a quiet overrun: the repository is public from the first commit, so progress is a thing you look at rather than a status we report. <!--tbf:link /process-->Process<!--/tbf:link--> describes the whole run.

### What do you need from me to start

What the thing has to do, who uses it, and what it has to talk to. Text and pictures can arrive later; the decisions cannot. Half an hour on <!--tbf:link /contact-->contact<!--/tbf:link--> is usually enough to find out whether we are the right people for it, and hearing that we are not is a normal outcome.

## What you get

### Do I own what you build for me

Your content, your data, your domain and your credentials are yours and always were. The code is published under an open licence, which gives you every right that matters in practice: run it, change it, hand it to another developer, keep it running without us. Nothing has to be bought back later, and <!--tbf:link /licenses-->licenses<!--/tbf:link--> lists the exact terms.

### Can you keep it private

No. Everything built here is published as open source, and agreeing to that is the condition of working with us. There is no version with an exception, a private repository, or a delay until later. The public repository can live under your own GitHub account or under ours; we recommend ours, where it sits beside the packages it is built from and goes through the same checks as everything else we run. Your content, your data and your credentials stay private; the code that runs them is public. <!--tbf:link /philosophy-->Philosophy<!--/tbf:link--> explains why that line sits where it does.

### Who maintains it afterwards

You, us, or anybody else you choose. Maintenance is optional and priced on its own, and nothing is held back to make it necessary. If you take the whole thing to another developer, the licence already allows it and the code is already in your hands.

## How it is built

### Will it run on Windows

Anything built for a browser works wherever a browser does, including on Windows and on a Mac. Desktop software written here runs on Linux and is not ported, at any price. <!--tbf:link /philosophy-->Philosophy<!--/tbf:link--> explains why, and <!--tbf:link /standards-->standards<!--/tbf:link--> states it as a requirement rather than a preference.

### What stops it from being sloppy

A program, not a promise. Types have to check, a discipline tool has to approve the shape of the code, the verification scripts have to run, and the whole thing has to build; work that fails any of them does not land. <!--tbf:link /standards-->Standards<!--/tbf:link--> lists what every change has to pass and <!--tbf:link /handbook-->handbook<!--/tbf:link--> describes how the work is built and released.

### What happens if Trebired disappears

Nothing of yours goes with it. Everything is already published under a licence that cannot be withdrawn, on other people's disks, and your own copy keeps working whether this organization exists or not. <!--tbf:link /governance-->Governance<!--/tbf:link--> says who maintains what while it does exist.

## Privacy and the open side

### Do you track visitors

No analytics, no advertising pixels, no behavioural profiling and no third party watching the reader on anything the organization runs. The <!--tbf:link /cookies-->cookie policy<!--/tbf:link--> lists what a browser stores, and it is short for that reason.

### What do you keep about me

What you send and what a server has to process to answer a request, and nothing gathered on the side. The <!--tbf:link /privacy-->privacy policy<!--/tbf:link--> names each one, how long it is kept, and how to ask for it back or have it deleted.

### Can I use your code in my own work

Yes, under the licence of the repository it came from, without asking and without owing anything. What the licence does not grant is the name: presenting your work as Trebired, or as endorsed by it, is the one thing it does not allow. <!--tbf:link /brand-->Brand<!--/tbf:link--> and the <!--tbf:link /trademark-->trademark policy<!--/tbf:link--> say exactly where that line is, and <!--tbf:link /contribute-->contribute<!--/tbf:link--> covers sending something back.
