## Reporting a vulnerability

Write to security@trebired.com with what you found, where you found it, and what an attacker could do with it. A proof of concept is worth more than a scanner report.

Do not open a public issue for a security problem. Everything Trebired publishes is open source, so an issue is a disclosure.

## What you receive

An acknowledgement within two working days, from a person rather than a template.

An assessment within a week, stating whether we agree it is a vulnerability and what we intend to do about it.

A fix released as soon as it is ready, and credit in the release notes if you want it.

## What we ask while you test

Test against your own installation where you can. Our code is public, so you can run it yourself.

Do not access, copy, modify, retain, or exfiltrate data that is not yours, and stop as soon as you have confirmed the issue.

Give us a reasonable window to fix the problem before you publish. We will not ask you to stay quiet indefinitely, and we will not use the window to argue the finding away.

## Scope

In scope: every service Trebired operates and every repository Trebired publishes.

Out of scope: reports that restate a scanner's output without an exploit behind them, missing hardening headers with no demonstrated impact, social engineering of the people who run the organization, denial of service by volume, and defects in third-party services we merely use.

## What we will not do

We will not threaten a reporter who followed this policy, and we will not treat good-faith research as an attack.

We run no bug bounty and pay no rewards. We say so here rather than letting anyone find out after the work is done.
